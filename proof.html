Proof

The method was earned, not designed.

Twelve years of infrastructure development, then a three-day automation build under a deadline that could not be met by hand. The projects below are not illustrations of the methodology. They are where it came from, and what it became.

Part one

Where the method came from.

Site development is an adversarial, documentation-heavy discipline with public hearings at the end of it. It rewards exactly one thing: knowing what is true before you have to defend it in front of a room.

Speed under deadline

400 sites, 60 days, three days to build the answer.

A national wireless carrier issued a portfolio of nearly 400 relocation candidates and gave 60 days to return feasibility and permitting recommendations on every one of them. Assembling that by hand was not achievable in the time available.

The carrier's site database was exported as the single source of truth. 315 candidates were plotted as geospatial points. Python generated review radii sized by market classification — half a mile urban, one mile rural. A macro-enabled Word template built the folder structure and a pre-populated feasibility analysis for every site.

Each generated document arrived already carrying its site number and name, state and jurisdiction, coordinates, ground elevation, existing tower height and structure type, carrier centerline and height above ground, and review radius — plus structured sections for search-area feasibility, permitting paths split between raw land and colocation, feasibility maps, and the relevant zoning ordinance excerpts.

317 feasibility packages across 316 sites, delivered on schedule. The pipeline was built in three days by an operator with no prior programming experience.

Only the attributes the automation actually required left the client's system: coordinates, structure height, city, state. Nothing else was exported.

Mapped to the method

  1. Source of truth. The carrier's own database, exported once, treated as authoritative.
  2. Structured operating data. 315 candidates as geospatial points with market-classified review radii.
  3. Workflows and controls. Radius generation and document assembly automated, with one template governing every output.
  4. Tested and handed off. 317 packages delivered against a fixed deadline.

Influence

Documents that became the standard.

Two national carriers independently took documentation built for internal workflow and adopted it as their own enterprise standard.

One deployed the feasibility and site documentation forms across its national vendor base. The other adopted a site candidate information package as its internal standard. Neither was pitched a product. Both encountered a working document and made it the bar their other vendors had to meet.

The follow-on work proved the forms were doing real work rather than formatting it. Quality review of other vendors' submittals against those same forms reached different feasibility conclusions — and recovered sites those reviews had ruled out.

Both carriers are named on request.

Mapped to the method

  1. Source of truth. One controlled form defining what a complete site record contains.
  2. Structured operating data. The same fields, in the same order, on every submittal from every vendor.
  3. Workflows and controls. A review standard that could be applied against work the author did not produce.
  4. Tested and handed off. Adopted enterprise-wide by two organizations, and operated by their vendors rather than by its author.

When the system meets an adversary

Two negotiations nobody expected to win.

Systems thinking is easy to demonstrate on tidy problems. These two were not tidy.

The blanket easement. A FirstNet parcel everyone had written off. A Canadian wind energy company had told the landowner its easement would cover fifteen feet on either side of its trunk line, then recorded a blanket easement across the entire eighty-acre parcel — rendering the property unleasable. Rather than move the search ring, the issue was worked across an international border until the developer agreed to re-survey and amend the agreement down to its actual footprint. Title cleared, ground lease secured, tower permitted and built.

The contested hearing. A national tower company learned a competing permit application was being filed and sent outside counsel to argue against it. Every hearing material — decks, exhibits, supporting findings — was built in house. The application was approved. Against retained counsel, without a law degree.

An operations practice that only demonstrates tidy process reads as theoretical. These are what happens when the process meets someone trying to defeat it.

Part two

What the method became.

The relocation automation was the hinge. It happened inside the infrastructure work, and the software practice came out of it. What follows was built after October 2024 by the same operator, using the same four steps, and all of it is running now.

Seven operational systems, in daily production use

Built for a multi-property operator: expense reporting with bank-statement import, receipt OCR and a quality gate that blocks submission on failure, in production since the end of May 2026; a property and location directory that every other system resolves against; linen inventory across five property trackers with a controlled master catalog; a mobile consumables inspection application with photographic evidence and duplicate-proof submission; a production inspection tracker in daily use; a turnover and issue tracker; and an authenticated employee portal that ties them together.

Engineering controls: versioned source packages, schema versioning, staged QA gates, deployment preflight, rollback planning, stable identifiers, protected formula columns, approval logs with approver and timestamp.

Operational result: a five-person team runs its month on these systems, without a technical specialist among them. The portal is live behind Workspace sign-in, and its protected interfaces fail closed to anonymous requests.

Production recovery, not just production

A field inspection committed successfully, then a slow path returned an error to the operator. The wrong move is telling a user to retry — that is how you get a duplicate record in a system people trust.

What happened instead: the transaction was identified by its original client submission ID, submission ID and payload hash, confirmed as already committed, and recovered without duplication.

Why it is here: a system is not proven by the release that went well. It is proven by what it does when something goes wrong and somebody has to be told the truth about it.

SAGE — local-first operations platform

A native macOS application for working across a knowledge base, projects and workspaces, meeting processing, review, backup and restore, with provider-flexible AI under human approval.

State: installed and in active internal use, in continued development, preparing for beta. Signed by Synergistic Solutions Unlimited. Its test suite is roughly the size of the application itself — a deliberate ratio, not an accident.

Not claimed: commercially available. It is not, and this page will say so until it is.

Lucid — native macOS writing application

An editor with an assistant surface that works inside other applications, supporting local models and multiple cloud providers, with credentials in the system Keychain and tracked changes on every suggestion.

State: shipped through a sequence of Apple-notarized, Gatekeeper-accepted releases, each one extraction-verified and installed. Not yet publicly distributed.

Operational result: in daily use by its author, which is how its defects get found — by use, not by a passing test.

What is not claimed

The boundaries are part of the evidence.

A portfolio page that only contains victories is not a portfolio page, it is advertising. So: a technology-transfer program is designed and not yet delivered. The current internal builds of both native applications are signed but await notarization before anyone outside receives them.

Every figure above is either exact or labelled as an estimate. Where a number does not exist — hours saved, user counts, precise production dates — none is offered, because the alternative is a figure that cannot survive being checked. That is the same standard applied to client work, and it is the reason the delivery claims can be taken at face value.
