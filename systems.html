Systems in practice

Practical operating systems, built from the work outward.

SSU connects data, field actions, review, automation, and documentation so recurring work becomes easier to operate and safer to change. The system families below are examples of the operating systems SSU can support—patterns we have developed and hardened, described here without client-specific detail.

01

Property, location, and asset registry

The operating problem

Locations, buildings, units, owners or entities, statuses, and resources are described differently in every tracker, form, and folder—so every new tool starts from scratch and drifts out of sync.

The system

A normalized upstream directory for properties, locations, buildings, units or listings, owner or entity records, status, operational eligibility, folder references, platform IDs, tracker templates, and created tracker instances. Identity and relationships live in one controlled structure and feed downstream systems.

The outcome

Establish one dependable operating record for the locations, assets, relationships, and resources that every downstream workflow depends on.

Designed for continuity: stable identifiers—not names, URLs, or row positions—act as permanent keys, so a name, label, folder, or interface can change without breaking every downstream workflow.

02

Inventory and replenishment operations

The operating problem

Counts, receipts, and restock decisions live in texts, photos, and ad hoc spreadsheet edits, so nobody can say with confidence what is on hand, what was received, or what to buy next.

The system

Catalog layers for standard, seasonal, and location-specific items; stable item IDs; current inventory state; count events; receiving events; par levels; low-stock logic; restock status; purchasing queues; and audit history. Staff use simple count and receiving interfaces while formulas, state tables, catalog rules, and logs remain protected behind the scenes.

The outcome

Turn counts, receipts, and restock decisions into a reliable event history and a clear action queue.

Designed for continuity: every count and receipt becomes a durable event record with an identifier, source, timestamp, user or role, result, and status—current state is derived from the history without erasing what happened before.

03

Linen, equipment, and resource planning

The operating problem

Operating standards, location requirements, and purchasing data are rebuilt by hand every time they are needed, and each rebuild introduces new errors.

The system

A central item catalog, product identifiers, package data, costs, active standards, location requirements, configuration rules, par logic, counts, exceptions, and purchase summaries. The product catalog, operational standards, and location requirements are separate but connected, so requirements are recalculated rather than manually rebuilt.

The outcome

Connect operating standards, location requirements, current counts, and purchasing data in one traceable planning system.

Designed for continuity: standards and requirements reference stable item identifiers, so pricing, packaging, or catalog changes update the plan instead of invalidating it.

04

Inspections and issue escalation

The operating problem

Field inspections produce photos and messages, but findings lose their context, issues stall without owners, and nobody can show what was checked or what happened next.

The system

Reusable inspection templates, location applicability rules, field submissions, photo or evidence references, pass/fail results, issue records, severity, assignment, due dates, escalation, and closeout.

The outcome

Convert field inspections into durable records, accountable issue queues, and visible follow-through.

Designed for continuity: checklist wording can evolve while stable identifiers preserve reporting continuity across template versions.

05

Expense, adjustment, and reporting workflows

The operating problem

Recurring financial workflows depend on fragile spreadsheets where one stray edit breaks a calculation, and supporting documents are scattered across inboxes and folders.

The system

Controlled input zones, durable formulas, folder dependencies, supporting-document routing, owner or client adjustments, review states, dashboards, and release-safe automation. Financial workflows get visible rules, clear evidence, and safe correction paths.

The outcome

Structure recurring financial and document workflows so inputs, calculations, reviews, and supporting evidence remain understandable.

Designed for continuity: input zones are separated from formula zones, review states are explicit, and corrections follow a safe, recorded path instead of silent edits.

06

AI-assisted project control

The operating problem

AI tools are useful in the moment, but chat transcripts are not a system of record—context evaporates between sessions, and unverified output can quietly become “truth.”

The system

A governed project memory and execution layer with source hierarchy, current state, decisions, queues, handoffs, QA records, activity logs, tool registries, source-grounded retrieval, and clear boundaries among AI agents and human authority. AI contributes to work without becoming an unverified source of truth.

The outcome

Give teams and AI tools the context, authority rules, and evidence needed to continue complex work without starting over.

Designed for continuity: durable decisions are promoted into controlled sources with evidence labels and readback requirements—no chat transcript or model memory automatically becomes truth, and consequential actions stay under human review.

Built to evolve

Not trapped in the first tool.

SSU may use spreadsheets, scripts, cloud drives, forms, lightweight web interfaces, local knowledge tools, or other practical components—whatever fits the operation today. The tool is a starting point, not a ceiling.

A spreadsheet can be a useful operating layer without becoming a permanent limitation. Stable identifiers, normalized tables, event histories, explicit roles, and documented interfaces allow today’s practical system to become tomorrow’s application without rebuilding the operating logic from memory.

Systems designed to evolve without discarding their history.
